# Syllabus Notes Relevant to the AI Project

Course: Separation Processes, CHE 4061-001, Fall 2026, University of Cincinnati
Instructor: Prof. Carlos Co (Assoc. Professor). Meets M/W 11:00 AM.
Textbook: Seader, Henley, Roper, *Separation Process Principles with Applications Using Process Simulators*, 4th ed. (Wiley).

## Course philosophy (paraphrased from the syllabus)
- **Problem-first teaching:** introduce a real-world problem first, then the tools to solve it, the way practicing engineers learn on the job. AI makes custom problem-first "textbooks" possible.
- **One model critiquing another:** the syllabus states that frontier agentic AI tools, especially when one model deeply critiques another model's results, are practically infallible on undergraduate ChemE problems. This is the logic behind requirement #5.
- **"WHAT IF?"** is the question an engineer should never stop asking; any calculation worth doing once gets repeated for different conditions.
- **"No Plot = No Pay!"** Every professional engineering calculation needs graphical visualization.
- The course is shifting from hand calculation and coding toward conceptual understanding, technical communication, and advanced topics.

## Course modules
1. Absorption/stripping: mass transfer coefficients, correlations, Kremser equation
2. VLE: ideal and non-ideal systems, flash calculations
3. Distillation: McCabe-Thiele, degrees of freedom, Fenske/Underwood/Gilliland
4. Advanced topics: distillation column dynamics, rigorous MESH, N2/O2/Ar cryogenic air separation, **crystallization**

The MVR crystallizer project connects to module 4 (crystallization), module 2 (VLE: boiling point elevation and evaporation are single-stage vapor-liquid separations), and the compression and energy-recovery discussion from the air separation lectures.
