How the explorer works

1. Overview

The explorer compares a tray column and a packed column doing the same dilute gas-absorption duty. The four terminal compositions are visible on one y–x diagram. The user directly drags y_out and x_out; the overall solute balance then determines the liquid-to-gas ratio and liquid flow.

The two designs are deliberately presented as two descriptions of the same separation. The tray side resolves the duty into equilibrium stages; the packed side resolves it into overall gas transfer units.

2. Model assumptions

  1. Dilute solute: mole fractions are used and L and V remain constant.
  2. The system is isothermal and isobaric.
  3. Equilibrium is represented by a straight line, y* = m·x, through the origin.
  4. The contacting is counter-current: gas moves upward and liquid moves downward.
  5. The tray design uses equilibrium stages corrected by one overall efficiency E_o.
  6. H_OG is treated as constant over the packed height.

These assumptions are what make the MVP closed-form and hand-checkable. The SO₂ preset intentionally demonstrates where the straight-line assumption becomes a weak representation of the real system.

3. Why L is derived, not entered

The overall solute balance is V(y_in − y_out) = L(x_out − x_in). Therefore L/V = (y_in − y_out)/(x_out − x_in).

The interface is intentionally built around the two dragged terminal points. There is no independent L input. This is central to the teaching interaction: moving x_out is the act of selecting the liquid rate.

4. Operating and equilibrium lines

The equilibrium relation is y* = m·x. The operating line is y = (L/V)(x − x_in) + y_out. At the current endpoints it passes through (x_in, y_out) and (x_out, y_in).

For absorption the operating line lies above the equilibrium line when the duty is feasible. The two endpoint driving forces are Δ_top = y_out − m·x_in and Δ_bot = y_in − m·x_out.

The MVP treats the separation as feasible exactly when both endpoint driving forces are positive. That means A < 1 can be feasible; an A ≥ 1 rule would add a constraint that the model does not specify.

5. Tray-column calculation

Kremser stage count

Define the absorption factor A = (L/V)/m and R = (y_in − m·x_in)/(y_out − m·x_in). For A ≠ 1 the theoretical stage count is N = ln[1 + (A−1)R]/ln(A) − 1. At A = 1 the limiting result is N = R − 1.

Fractional-stage staircase

A stage is one horizontal move to equilibrium followed by a vertical move to the operating line. The stage recursion is geometric away from A = 1. Therefore the last fractional stage is logarithmic, not a linear interpolation of y. The implementation computes N analytically and draws the final staircase segment truncated at y_in.

Actual trays and height

Actual trays are N_act = ceil(N/E_o), followed by Z_tray = N_act·S + h_top + h_bot. The current project intentionally uses this as a convention for installed height; it is not presented as a universal geometric truth.

6. Packed-column calculation

The overall-gas transfer-unit number is N_OG = ∫ dy/(y − y*). Substituting the linear operating relation gives a closed-form Colburn expression. The packed height is simply Z = H_OG·N_OG.

The implementation also evaluates the same integral with Simpson's rule and with a log-mean driving force calculation. These are cross-checks against the analytic expression rather than alternative user inputs.

7. HETP connection

Because the analytic expressions share the same numerator, HETP = H_OG·g(A), where g(A) = A ln(A)/(A−1). Near A = 1, the implementation uses the prescribed series g ≈ 1 + (A−1)/2 − (A−1)²/6.

At A = 1, HETP = H_OG. The balanced demo is included specifically so that the removable singularity and its physical interpretation can be seen directly.

8. Clamping and pinches

State owns every clamp. The y_out lower bound is the top thermodynamic pinch; the x_out upper bound is the bottom thermodynamic pinch. The opposite two bounds are UI range limits rather than thermodynamic pinches.

That distinction matters pedagogically. The visible drawing and numerical state always use the same already-clamped values.

9. Staged versus continuous contacting

A tray column resolves the absorber into discrete contacting stages. Its composition profile therefore has jumps between tray levels, and the installed tray count is an integer. A packed column represents differential mass transfer continuously along the bed, so its profile varies smoothly and its height is continuous.

The project does not claim that one contacting method is universally preferred. Selection depends on the wider process design and on constraints not included in this MVP. Future phase F-3 in the project plan proposes a separate teaching exercise using scenarios such as fouling, low pressure drop, vacuum operation, side draws, and turndown.

10. The SO₂ teaching case

SO₂–air–water is included to show a model limitation. The preset uses a large m value and an illustrative straight-line fit, while the real equilibrium is curved over appreciable composition ranges. The site therefore presents the SO₂ numbers as illustrative of the method rather than as a process design.
